Cindy and I started a garden in our backyard with very poor soil. As we amended the soil over time, the garden bed became raised, leading to water pooling during heavy rains and swamping the vegetables.

In 2023, I addressed this by digging a deep trench through the garden and burying a 12-inch culvert to allow water to pass underneath. On the downhill side, the water flowed into a narrow ditch and continued its path.

In August 2025, Cindy and I attended a rain garden workshop with Linda and Casey. Inspired by the workshop, I decided to build a rain garden—designed to capture runoff in a low spot, creating a wetland-like area for moisture-loving plants—on our property. This article documents the beginning of that project and will be updated as the rain garden matures.

### Step 1: Digging the Basin

I began by excavating an area on the downhill side of the culvert. Using the front-end loader on my small tractor, I scraped away a significant amount of clay soil to form a depression a few inches lower than the culvert outlet.

### Step 2: Lining with Stone

Next, I lined the basin with stone. I sourced rocks from a neighbor’s driveway project, where I helped remove approximately 300 large stones. Some went to Pat’s house, others to Todd’s pond-building efforts, and the rest I stockpiled for myself. One large rectangular sandstone slab became the overflow outlet; when the rain garden fills, excess water will spill over this stone onto the surrounding grass.

### Step 3: Installing the Pipe

To manage water flow, I placed a smaller pipe inside the culvert with the end turned upward. This setup allows water to flow through the culvert without completely bypassing the rain garden, enabling the depression to gradually saturate while preventing flooding. I set the pipe’s outlet just below the level of the sandstone overflow, ensuring excess water escapes once the basin is full.

### Step 4: Adding Soil

With the boulders and pipe in place, I backfilled the depression with compost and topsoil. This created a planting area ready for moisture-loving plants that will help the rain garden thrive.

### Planting the Rain Garden

Choosing the right plants is crucial for a successful rain garden. They must tolerate both wet conditions after a storm and dry periods between rains. I plan to use a mix of native flowers, grasses, and shrubs for resilience and habitat value.

#### Suggested Plants for a Rain Garden

🌿 **Grasses & Sedges** (structure and root depth)

* Switchgrass (Panicum virgatum)
* Fox Sedge (Carex vulpinoidea)
* Little Bluestem (Schizachyrium scoparium)

🌸 **Perennials** (color and pollinator value)

* Blue Flag Iris (Iris versicolor)
* Purple Coneflower (Echinacea purpurea)
* Black-eyed Susan (Rudbeckia hirta)
* Swamp Milkweed (Asclepias incarnata) – *great for monarch butterflies*

🌳 **Shrubs** (height and wildlife cover)

* Red Twig Dogwood (Cornus sericea)
* Buttonbush (Cephalanthus occidentalis)
* Winterberry (Ilex verticillata)

I will start small, planting a few species and observing their response to the conditions. Over time, the rain garden should develop into a self-sustaining wetland habitat that effectively manages runoff and supports pollinators, birds, and other wildlife.
