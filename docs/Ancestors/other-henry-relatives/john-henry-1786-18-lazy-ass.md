John Henry was born in 1796 and had just reached adulthood when his family moved to their new home in Ohio. According to tradition, he had earlier been chosen by John Parsons of Enfield, Connecticut—his mother Rhoda’s father—to return east with him from Berkshire and attend school there. This arrangement eased his mother’s heavy responsibilities while giving added opportunity to one of her most promising children.
Later, in 1813, while John’s father was serving in the General Court, John himself was attending a Latin school near Boston. During that time, he witnessed the enthusiastic reception given to Commodore Bainbridge when he arrived in February aboard his flagship, the Constitution, after capturing the British frigate Java. Stirred by this display of military glory—and teased by his brothers with the remark that “one small head could carry all he knew”—John ran away at seventeen and enlisted in the Third United States Artillery in Hudson, New York.
His military career was short-lived. His mother pressed her husband relentlessly for several months until he secured John’s release through a writ of habeas corpus on the grounds that he was underage. Because his uniform bore the initials “L.A.” (Light Artillery), his soldier brothers mockingly nicknamed the studious and physically inactive youth “Lazy Ass.” The name never caught on, however. In fact, when their father later instructed Orrin, the oldest brother, to take one of the boys ahead of the family to Ohio, Orrin chose John as his companion and coworker—evidence of the real respect in which he was held—as they undertook the difficult task of building a home in the wilderness.
Once the family had settled in Ohio, John Henry found relief from the exhausting labor of clearing land by teaching school during the winter. While teaching in Canfield, Ohio—about forty miles southeast, in a community where he was chosen to instruct advanced students, some already experienced teachers—he met one of his pupils, Polly Jaqua. On July 1, 1819, he married her. Polly was the seventh child of Captain Simon Jaqua and his wife, Ruth (Hanchet).
