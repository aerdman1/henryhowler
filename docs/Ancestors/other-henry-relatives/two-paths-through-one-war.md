In the summer and fall of 1777, the Revolutionary War reached directly into Blanche’s family tree. On her mother Effie’s side was **Lewis Hatch**, a third great-grandfather who served as a Patriot in the Massachusetts militia. In September, he marched to join General Gates’s army as British forces under General Burgoyne pushed south from Canada. Though Hatch never fought in the main battles, he helped guard key river crossings near Saratoga—quiet but essential work that helped trap the British army and force its surrender.

On Blanche’s father Frederick’s side was her third great-grandfather **Jehiel Hawley**, a founder of Arlington, Vermont, who chose a very different path. Hawley remained loyal to the British Crown and fought on the British side at the Battle of Bennington. After that defeat, he was arrested and briefly imprisoned by Patriot authorities. Released because of his long-standing reputation for fairness, Hawley found he could no longer safely remain at home. He joined Burgoyne’s army, where he served in a position of trust, known for showing mercy even during bitter times.

When Burgoyne surrendered at Saratoga, Hatch returned home with the militia, his service complete. Hawley, however, chose exile rather than abandon his beliefs. He set out for Canada with family members under harsh conditions but died along the way, far from the town he helped build. Through these two men—one Patriot, one Loyalist—Blanche inherited a Revolutionary legacy shaped by courage, conscience, and deeply personal choices made during one of the most decisive moments in American history.
