The Akron Beacon Journal, Sunday, May 28, 1961 — Page 53

**Just Call Henry ‘Mr. Hiram’**

By Robert Hennerty
Beacon Journal Staff Writer

HIRAM — They went to a lot of trouble here Saturday to honor one of the worst head football coaches Hiram College has ever had.

But Charles A. “Chuck” Henry is not called “Mr. Hiram” on the basis of his coaching prowess. His team scored only one touchdown and lost all its games in 1931 — Henry’s only year as mentor.

When 500 alumni and friends gave the 65-year-old Henry a rousing ovation at a dinner in his honor Saturday night, it was out of respect for a man only as deep perhaps more than any other person for his tradition-steeped alma mater.

Henry retires July 1 after 31 years as alumni secretary and college fund-raiser par excellence.

It’s typical of Henry that retirement is only a word. He’ll continue on a limited basis, he says — handling one of the nation’s more successful alumni gift programs.

The tall, soft-spoken ex-coach built his school’s alumni fund so well that this Spring’s report from the American Alumni Council placed Hiram sixth among 242 liberal arts colleges in total giving.

Henry would hand the credit for this to generous graduates, but honors heaped on him by his college and the Alumni Council tell a different story.

The Hiram Board of Trustees seven years ago granted him the seldom-conferred Special Award for Distinguished Service for his leadership during the 1954 capital campaign to finance construction of a physical education building.

The American Alumni Council honored him with its 25-year Service Award in 1955.

Henry can trace his Hiram ties to the days when “grandpappy” headed here.

“Grandpappy” was Charles Henry, who studied here under James Garfield, then followed that most distinguished Hiram president into the Civil War.

Henry returned from battle to become a Hiram trustee about the same time Garfield became the 20th President of the United States.

Chuck’s father, Frederick “Judge” Henry, kept the Hiram tradition alive. A practicing attorney and jurist in Cleveland, he was also a Hiram graduate and Board of Trustees member.

Then along came Chuck.

He made a big name as an undergrad, as Hiram’s star tackle and first representative on the All-Ohio football team. That was in 1916, the best year the college ever had on the gridiron.

He was graduated from Hiram and Ohio State University (with an agriculture degree) in 1920, after a stint in World War I.

From 1924 to 1930, Henry was assistant football coach. He likes to remember those years:

“I will be glad to admit I was the best assistant coach in football, but a hell of a head coach.”

Henry served in the dual capacity of director of admissions and alumni secretary from 1930 to 1943. He relinquished the admissions job then to Aaron H. Kelker, who is now following his longtime friend into the alumni secretary’s post.

Kelker also will take over as alumni notes editor for a college magazine replacing “The Broadcaster,” a publication which Henry put to bed for 31 years.

The Henrys are now a fourth-generation Hiram family. All four of Chuck’s sons — Frederick of Cleveland Heights, Charles and John of Chagrin Falls, and Donald of Geauga Lake — are graduates. So is Chuck’s wife, Blanche.

They live in Bainbridge, not far from the Geauga County farm where his grandfather settled.

When the fifth Henry generation heads for classes in this sleepy little Portage County village (Chuck and Blanche have 13 grandchildren, some in high school) they’ll find a permanent memorial to their grandfather.

That was one of the big surprises Saturday night — the announcement that Hiram’s outdoor sports field henceforth will be played on “Charles A. Henry Athletic Field.”
