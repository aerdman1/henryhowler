I always wanted four kids. I grew up in a family whose entire existence was based on children. My mom was a nationally known poet, an author, and a public speaker. Her poems were about the humorous side of raising kids! For example, one of her first poems was called "Fast Burn!": “If ever a morning should flower, with one fine harmonious hour, someone will spoil it by flushing the toilet while Daddy is taking a shower!”


My mom was so into writing poetry that one morning she strapped me on the toilet and forgot where she put me! After searching the neighborhood with the entire Cleveland Heights Fire and Police Departments, one of the officers found me asleep on the toilet. So what did Mom do? She wrote a poem called “Scotty on the Potty” and read it to the whole world! It was very embarrassing! I always felt that anyone who could make a career out of writing about toilets, let alone finding something to rhyme with one, had to be not only clever, but a positive thinker.


My aunt, Mom’s younger sister, with a little help from Big Bird and Oscar the Grouch, started the educational television station Channel 25 WVIZ TV. I also have a sister who started the local Therapeutic Riding Center. We have thirteen teachers in the family, a juvenile court judge, and way too many kids.


So, when I was 48 and my first wife changed her mind, deciding that two cats were enough, I had to make a move. When Michelle, along with her two sons, offered me her hand as a 50th birthday present, I thought my life was complete. But, be careful what you wish for! Chelle said she would give me one of “my own” if I promised her a daughter. I did, and after eating lots of brussel sprouts, I came through and she delivered! A girl! And ten minutes later, another boy!
So in one year, at 51, I got what I always wanted, or so I thought! Having kids at this age does have its benefits. Financially, I think you're more likely to be “settled in” money-wise, and there’s a good chance that my life insurance will cover their college. Emotionally, I worry about not waking up, but I hear from younger dads that they worry about the opposite. On another positive note, there’s a good chance I won’t have to give my daughter away.


But physically, there are some drawbacks. I had to get laser treatment so I could play ball with the boys, and my 6-year-old daughter can run faster than me. All in all, I would say that having four kids about ten years earlier might have had some advantages, but it wouldn’t have been a miracle. I still feel I’m the luckiest man alive! Hence, 2MNY2L8… it’s just the humorous side of having kids.


Thanks, Moms!
Scott Henry


June 6, 2008
Linda
