This page compiles email updates from Brian, forwarded by Dodie to Linda, chronicling Brian's journey from finishing his job in India, through Borneo, and back home in 2011.

## April 11, 2011 – Heading Back West 2

*(Note: Original content had "2001", corrected to "2011" for chronological consistency with other entries.)*

**From Dodie:** Last email from Brian on his adventure. He should be arriving in LA this morning.

When I arrived in Kuala Lumpur, I had to take an hour-long bus ride into the city to find a hotel. On the bus I met a little boy and his energetic younger brother. They were traveling the world with their parents, originally from New Zealand, currently living in Bangladesh, and soon to be moving to Vietnam.

The older boy—only in 6th grade—spoke three languages (French, English, and Arabic). The strangest part was that his little brother was exactly six years younger, and their parents were both teachers. Sound familiar?

I offered a woman my seat when the bus filled up, but she declined. Later, she thanked me again and even offered me a place to stay with her family for the night. I politely declined and headed toward the Meridien Hotel to splurge a little.

I spent most of the day by the pool and ordered room service. The Gerardo area there is beautiful, with two waterfalls and a waterslide winding through faux rocks.

Tonight, I’ll be in Mumbai around midnight and will write again.

Love you,\
Brian

## April 8, 2011 – Heading Back West

In a few hours I begin my trip back west. Tonight, I fly into Kuala Lumpur, where I’ll have a day-long layover before heading back to Mumbai.

I don’t look forward to returning to India—everything there is complicated, from getting a taxi to airport security checks. It’s frustrating. Employees will sometimes hold you for an hour just because they can—Jess found that out last time she was here.

I’m hoping for a smoother process this time. I’ll write again when I reach KL.

Got a flight and a meal to catch—\
Love you,
Brian

## April 7, 2011 – Borneo Update

Just got back from an island off the coast of Kota Kinabalu—Mamutik.

When I reached the island, I trekked into the jungle for an hour before finding my campsite. There was an old abandoned lintu overlooking the ocean, with crystal-clear water surrounded by tropical wilderness.

The first night went well. I set up my hammock under the lintu, using an extra rain fly I bought in KK. I cooked my own food, swam in the reef, and saw all sorts of brightly colored fish. No jellyfish, thankfully.

That night, I fell asleep to the sound of waves crashing and strange animals calling from the forest. Two hours later, I awoke to a tropical thunderstorm blowing sideways through camp—this time from the northwest, not the southwest as I’d prepared for. I scrambled out of my hammock, accidentally kicked down my rain fly support, and spent a frantic few minutes wrestling my setup back together. Thankfully, I moved fast enough that nothing got soaked, and I eventually got back to sleep.

The second night was similar, with another storm—though this time I was better prepared.

The next morning, I sat on a beach waiting for my prearranged boat. Tonight, I’m full of Italian food and happily staying dry in a hotel. Tomorrow is my last day in Borneo; I leave at 8 p.m. for Kuala Lumpur, then onward to India and home.

I’m trying to arrange a morning of whitewater rafting or windsurfing before I go.

Miss you,\
Love Brian

## April 4, 2011 – Kapit – Myrimar – Bakun – Belaga – Miri

**From Dodie:** “Hadn’t heard from him for a while, but got this early this morning. He heads back to Mumbai April 9, spends the night, leaves for LA on the 10th, and arrives on the 11th. Quite a trip!”

Just crawled out of the jungle—hot, sticky, but smiling ear to ear. I’m in Miri now, after a bus ride from the Niah Caves.

Traveling in rural Borneo requires patience. Some days there’s no boat or ATV, and you wait until the next. The river may be too low until the dam releases water in the afternoon. Sometimes transportation never comes, and you have to walk all day.

I’ve been trying to find a word to describe the people of Sarawak. “Nice” is too mild. “Sweet” comes closest. Everyone has been so helpful—guiding me, inviting me in, feeding me meals without asking anything in return.

Adventures these past days include:

* Staying in a traditional longhouse and later a logging camp
* A wild 4WD expedition through jungle mountains
* A long-distance boat ride across a lake “the size of Singapore”
* Eating smoked catfish with a shop owner
* Exploring the Niah Caves
* Sprinting through jungle to make a boat on time
* Staying in a hostel in Batu Niah

Today I’ll decide whether to fly to the Kelabit Highlands or Kota Kinabalu. I’m glad to be back in civilization, but I already miss the river.

Will write again soon—\
Love Brian

## March 29, 2011 – Sibu to Kapit

Mom, I spent my youth running around the woods—I have confidence. But I also know too much confidence can be dangerous.

So much has happened since my last message. After leaving the internet café, I came across a Buddhist temple with a seven-story pagoda. A very enthusiastic monk asked if I was interested in Taoism—I said yes. He taught me about yin and yang, the heavens, earth, and elements. I practiced Chinese calligraphy, had tea and bread, and in the end, he literally handed me a key to climb the pagoda. All he asked was that I email Lonely Planet about him.

Later, I bought a Malaysian fishing rod, lures, and gear. The next morning, I caught the 5 a.m. boat to Kapit, riding on the roof as the sun rose over misty jungle mountains.

After checking into a grimy hotel (dead cockroaches, stained bed, but at least air-conditioning), I searched for someone to take me upriver to a longhouse. Eventually, a fisherman took me to a waterfall where I swam and fished. Very beautiful—photos will say more.

Tomorrow, I’ll try to reach Belaga if river levels allow. Otherwise, I may connect with a guide I just bumped into—he’s even listed in Lonely Planet!

Wish you were here.\
Love,
Brian

## March 28, 2011 – Kuching and Sibu

I arrived in Kuching early yesterday. With no boats running to Sibu, I detoured to Bako National Park.

The hike was unforgettable: boardwalks, thick jungle, monkeys crashing through trees, bamboo ladders leading down to a secluded white sand beach. I dove into the ocean before racing back to catch my boat. Easily the most intense hiking I’ve ever done.

Today I crossed the South China Sea to Sibu—five hours of beautiful scenery. Tomorrow I head up the Batang Rejang River toward Kapit, possibly staying with a local tribe in a longhouse.

Wish you were here.\
Love,
Brian

## March 28, 2011 – Hello from Kuala Lumpur

Jess, it’s like a grittier Singapore here. After a short sleep on the plane, I arrived at 8 a.m. KL time.

The transit system is confusing—everything has nicknames in Malay—so it took asking several people before I found my way to Chinatown. The hotel was decent, about $35 USD.

I explored markets (everything from scorpions to DVDs), temples, and the aviary—the world’s largest. Peacocks everywhere, and I was even attacked by a stork (don’t worry, I got it on video!).

Tomorrow, I head to Borneo. Looking forward to leaving the chaos of KL behind for the jungle.

Love,\
Brian
